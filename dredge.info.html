EXPLORING DREDGE

LET'S DIVE DEEPER INTO ITS ABYSMAL DEPTH

A Little Introduction

The title of the game that I’ve chosen is called Dredge. It was developed by Black Salt Games and published by Team17.
It became an instant hit for its genre - fishing, survival combined with horror.
It was first released on controller games on March 30, 2023.
The app version came later in 2025. It is a single-player fishing game with some dark twists of cosmic horror, from mutated fishes to mythical creatures. Dredge, unsurprisingly, was intended for people who love adventurous RPGs, simulators, and psychological, cozy horror. It’s marked E10+, which means only people of age 10 and older can play it.

Its Twisty Plots

We start with a fisherman who crashed his boat near the coastal town of Greater Marrow, where he decided to settle and worked as the town’s food provider. Everything was alright and normal at first, but then he begins to encounter some strange things happening, especially during the night: strange fog, rocks that suddenly appear from out of nowhere, ghost ships, mutated fish, and sea monsters. As he sails around the whole map, he finds messages in bottles that describe a journal entry of a woman named Julie. In these messages Julie recounts of her arrival with her husband to the islands here. As the story goes on, we also find out that Julie was actually our amnesiac fisherman’s wife. All along, he was trying to resurrect her using a cursed book only to find out that the person who was helping him was no one other than himself.

The (Main) Characters

Throughout the storyline, we find the Fisherman meeting many new, interesting people. Here are the main ones. To start off, the protagonist of our game is a fisherman named Morgan. He is the player character and the story of whom we’re exploring is his very own. The Collector comes after Morgan as the most important character in the plot. The Collector is a mysterious man who gives the Fisherman the task of bringing back 5 lost relics from all across the map. We later learn that as it turns out, the Collector is actually only a figment of imagination of the Fisherman’s mind. He came from Morgan’s own guilt and memories over his wife’s death. Speaking of his late wife, she is another main character, Julie, her name, is basically the emotional and narrative element that brings destruction to the entire plot. This was because Morgan was so desperate to resurrect her without caring for the consequences. The Mayor is the next one, who at first is not at all significant, but later becomes much more important. He is the ex-leader of the Greater Marrow who was beside the Fisherman and his wife when the Cursed Book of The Deep was first discovered. There are two more main characters left. These two are lesser known, but they are just as important. The Shipwright and the Fishmonger are the one who provide your gameplay services by repairing and upgrading your boats, while also buying your catches.

The Gameplay

Now that we’ve finished with the characters, we’ll move onto the gameplay of the game. Dredge’s gameplay is quite simple and straightforward, you sail, fish, and sometimes do minigames to discover new information. By succeeding in the minigames, you get to pull up long-lost relics and treasure. To make the journey easier, you can sell fish to earn money which can be used for upgrading your boat. Then there is the day-night cycle. The daytimes are filled with calmness and the “all is well” atmosphere, which the game has made to purposely trick us into thinking that all is safe. However, when day shifts to night, we learn that there are dangerous and mutated creatures out there in the dark. Sailing after dark can also raise your sanity level, something I find very good and fitting to put in, which triggers a panic meter where fog comes in, creepy sounds play, and hallucinations/mean sea monsters attack your ship.

Exploring the Map

According to Wikipedia, Dredge is built around a world of isolated archipelago (a cluttered of islands across the ocean). You start out in a relatively peaceful area called The Marrows where you just catch normal fish and get used to the gameplay. Everything seems alright at first, but as you explore farther, the map gets more dangerous. There are a total of 5 different areas that you, including the one I just mentioned. However if you get the DLCs, you will get 2 additional areas - The Pale Reach (icy setting) and The Iron Rig (oil drilling industry area). The Gale Cliffs is the next place that you should be going to if you’re following the storyline. A giant monster eel swims around in the Gale Cliffs during the nighttime for the purpose of hunting you and your boat down. It is basically a rocky maze. Next is the Stellar Basin, a pretty-looking area, with bioluminescent creatures that you can find at night. A kraken lives in the middle of a seemingly bottomless pit surrounded by glowing jellyfish. The Twisted Strand is the next destination - a mangrove maze filled with large trees and muddy water. Mindsuckers, worm-like monsters, can be found there. Finally, we meet the Unseeing Mother which can be found in the Devil’s Spine. The Unseeing Mother is a blind giant fish that hunts you using her children, Little Red Fish, of how they latch onto your boat and signal her. Next up is Dredge’s atmosphere, which is very unique compared to other games. It is a mix of cozy fishing simulator during the day plus a psychological cosmic horror during the night

Colours & Shades

Now, I will tell you the palette that Dredge colors use. This game uses muted maritime colours, hence what the game is all about. Upon a deeper look, we see that Dredge’s main colours are Navy (#122E34), Rusty Crimson Red (#8B2522), Magenta (#4A154B). Navy is for the ocean’s water and depth. Red is for the game’s accents and fisherman’s boat roof. Magenta is for the shade used for the aberrations and the night-time effects. These colours are appropriate for this video game because they are all relatively dark colours. If Dredge’s ocean was bright blue for example you would rather feel like you are on a relaxing vacation instead of sailing a boat in the middle of an abyssal depth. Cosmic horror is all about making you feel isolated and helpless in the big wide world after all. So navy is the right color, it looks deep, dark, and mysterious. Next is the red, a color that is universally symbolized for meaning of danger, distress, etc. Also in my opinion, they chose red for the boat’s color to make us stand out apart from the navy blue of the ocean. Red is also used for the panic eye meter in the game, which is fitting because Dredge is trying to tell the players that danger is all around. As it turns out, Magenta is very unique indeed, it actually doesn’t exist, but rather a colour that the brain created by blending blue and red together. The aberrations are considered “unnatural” so is the color Magenta, so they fit like peas in a pod. Now onto the next topic: Dredge’s fonts. According to Reddit, Dredge uses a font called Front Page Neue, colored it white and capitalize all, plus a little shadowing under the letters to make them pop. As the name of the font suggests, Front Page Neue is like a “look-over-here” kind of font - it catches your attention immediately. All contents want us to pay attention to them, not just Dredge, so they can get money from it. This font is also very minimalistic which goes great with the gothic comic book graphic style Dredge is trying to do. In addition, since Dredge made it white, white is a shade that is commonly known for popping against dark colors, and Dredge mainly uses dark colours in its game.

Extras (The Graphics & Animations)

Another point that I absolutely adore about Dredge is its animation and artwork of 3D elements combined with some 2Ds. The letters and dialogues are handwritten. In short, Dredge’s art style is low-poly. In my opinion, 3D mixed with some 2D art styles are the best, after all there is a combination, and difference that we can see clearly from both of them. Despite being two different dimensional graphics, 3D and 2D art styles blend really well together, creating a smooth effect of Comic-book style gothic illustrations - a contrast to the open ocean. What I find best of the animations are the jerky movements for mutating fish that shows their unnatural existence. Additionally to how bright daylight smoothly shifts into foggy nighttime. Now moving onto Dredge’s performance. Its graphics are generally good all across different platforms due to its light cell-shaded art style. In my experience, on a Nintendo Switch, it was very good, no glitches of any kind happened.

Why I like It

Now that you have learned about what Dredge basically is about, I will tell you about why it is my favourite game of all time. I first discovered Dredge while trying to find a good game to play on Nintendo on Google. I immediately knew right away that I would love this game, because before I had already played many different cozy horrors. At first I didn’t specifically expect any special qualities to Dredge, but later it became clearer that it stood out for me. This is due to the fact that unlike other cozy horrors, Dredge can actually be scary at times, at least in my opinion - when the monsters and other things jumpscare you. I have liked this game from the moment I started playing it only for just 5 minutes in 2025. The thing that keeps me most interested in Dredge is its plot twist, graphic, and content. I especially enjoyed the Resilient Traveller song because of its rhythmical accordion tunes. The Devil’s Spine is also my favourite area in the game. I like the idea of how the Unseeing Mother uses her children as her eyes. The character that I’d say I like best would actually be the player character, Morgan, because he’s the whole reason for the plot twist. I would recommend this game to basically anyone. I wouldn’t care if they dislike cozy horror, or just horror in general, I would still recommend it to them. The amazingness of this game cannot be ignored or overlooked. It is very underrated.
